# Training-Data Provenance, Knowledge Cutoff and Contamination Tests for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B

Research date: 2026-10-11. Labels used: **[V]** = verified this session against the primary source (quote or table read directly); **[BK]** = from background knowledge, not re-fetched this session (treat as unverified until checked); **[INF]** = my inference.

## Cutoffs: what "after the training cutoff" means for R1-Distill-Qwen-14B

### Takeaway
The weights come from two lineages. (1) Qwen2.5-14B pretraining: 18T tokens, released 19 Sep 2024, **no documented knowledge cutoff**. (2) An ~805k-sample SFT set generated by DeepSeek-R1, whose base DeepSeek-V3 has a **documented July 2024 knowledge cutoff**, and whose math SFT prompts are stated to come "exclusively from pre-2023 competitions". Nothing published after the model's release (20–22 Jan 2025) can be in the weights. A conservative safe boundary is **content first published after January 2025**. Content from Aug 2024 to Jan 2025 is very unlikely to be present but cannot be ruled out, because Qwen's own cutoff is undocumented.

### Cited Findings
- **Qwen2.5 release and data.** The Qwen2.5 blog announced the release on **September 19, 2024**. Base models were "pretrained on our latest large-scale dataset, encompassing up to 18 trillion tokens". The blog gives no knowledge cutoff. [V] — [Qwen2.5 blog](https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen2.5/)
- **Qwen2.5 technical report** (arXiv 2412.15115; v2 dated Jan 3, 2025):
  - pretraining data was scaled "from the previous 7 trillion tokens to 18 trillion tokens";
  - **no cutoff date stated**;
  - pretraining folds in "training data from Qwen2.5-Math and Qwen2.5-Coder";
  - decontamination removes a training sequence if |LCS| ≥ 13 and |LCS| ≥ 0.6 × min(len) against eval sequences;
  - math SFT uses "chain-of-thought data of Qwen2.5-Math, which encompasses … public datasets, K-12 problem collections, and synthetic problems". [V] — [arXiv 2412.15115](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15115)
- **Qwen2.5-Math** (arXiv 2409.12122):
  - Qwen Math Corpus v2 has ">1 trillion tokens" of math web text (Common Crawl), code, encyclopedias, exam questions and synthetic data generated by Qwen2-72B-Instruct;
  - post-training CoT data uses "GSM8K, MATH, and NuminaMath" plus exclusive Chinese K-12 problems;
  - decontamination uses 13-gram matching with an LCS ratio above 0.6, against GSM8K, MATH, Minerva Math, Gaokao 2023 En, OlympiadBench, College Math, MMLU STEM, GaoKao, CMATH, CN Middle School 24, **AIME 24 and AMC 23**;
  - no cutoff date stated. [V] — [arXiv 2409.12122](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12122)
- **Relevance of Qwen2.5-Math to the 14B model.** R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and 7B are built on Qwen2.5-Math. **R1-Distill-Qwen-14B is built on general Qwen2.5-14B**, per Table 6 of the R1 paper. Qwen2.5-Math data still enters indirectly, because the Qwen2.5 report says Qwen2.5-Math data was folded into pretraining and SFT. [V] — [arXiv 2501.12948v2 PDF, Table 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.12948v2); [arXiv 2412.15115](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15115)
- **DeepSeek-V3** (arXiv 2412.19437):
  - 14.8T pretraining tokens;
  - its 1.5M-instance SFT set includes reasoning data generated by R1;
  - the V3 report itself gives no cutoff. [V] — [arXiv 2412.19437](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19437)
- **DeepSeek-R1 paper, v2** (4 Jan 2026, the Nature version; Nature 645:633–638, 2025), decontamination appendix: "DeepSeek-V3 base has a knowledge cutoff date of **July 2024**, predating evaluation benchmarks like CNMO 2024, and we filtered out any text segments (including web pages and GitHub files) that contained matching **10-gram** sequences from evaluation questions or reference solutions… in the mathematics domain alone… removed approximately **six million** potential pre-training texts. For post-training, mathematical SFT data and RL training prompts were sourced exclusively from **pre-2023 competitions** and underwent the same n-gram filtering protocol…" It also states: "we acknowledge that the n-gram based decontamination method cannot prevent the paraphrase of testset. Therefore, it is possible that benchmarks released before 2024 may suffer from contamination issues." [V] — [arXiv 2501.12948v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.12948v2); [Nature DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09422-z)
- **V3-Base pretraining sources** (R1 v2): "we exclusively use plain web pages and e-books, without incorporating any synthetic data… some web pages contain a significant number of OpenAI-model-generated answers." [V] — [arXiv 2501.12948v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12948v2)
- **The 800k distillation SFT set** (R1 v2, §B.3.3 and Table 5):
  - about 600k reasoning samples come from rejection sampling on the first-stage RL checkpoint of R1. Some are graded by DeepSeek-V3 acting as a generative reward model. CoTs with mixed languages, long paragraphs or code blocks are filtered out;
  - about 200k non-reasoning samples reuse parts of the DeepSeek-V3 SFT set;
  - Table 5 breakdown: **Math 395,285**; Code 211,129; STEM 10,124; Logic 10,395; General 177,812; **Total 804,745**. Average length is 6,094 tokens for math and 5,355 overall;
  - "math-related data are primarily in Chinese and English… verifiable";
  - STEM and logic questions are "sourced from publicly available textbooks and online repositories";
  - code includes 5,151 Codeforces and 2,504 AtCoder problems. [V] — [arXiv 2501.12948v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.12948v2)
- **Distillation hyperparameters** (R1 v2, §B.4.3): 2–3 epochs on the 800k set, cosine decay to one-tenth of the initial LR, context length 32,768, batch size 64. The **14B model used initial LR 7e-5 on base Qwen2.5-14B**. [V] — [arXiv 2501.12948v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.12948v2)
- **Release dates.**
  - arXiv 2501.12948 v1 was posted **22 Jan 2025** [V] — [arXiv abs v2 page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948v2).
  - The weights and GitHub release came on **20 Jan 2025** [BK].
  - The model card names base Qwen2.5-14B, "800k samples curated with DeepSeek-R1", MIT license and no cutoff. It reports AIME 2024 pass@1 of 69.7 and MATH-500 of 93.9 for the 14B model. [V] — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B)
- **Post-cutoff evaluation by DeepSeek.** The paper uses AIME 2025 as its "unseen" generalization test: "variations of test set questions or discussions of related problems may still exist on websites that were included in the pre-training corpus". R1 scores 75% pass@1 on AIME 2025. [V] — [arXiv 2501.12948v2 §E.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.12948v2)

### Inferences
- [INF] **Latest plausible content date.** It is bounded by:
  - (a) the date the Qwen2.5 pretraining data was frozen, which is undocumented but must precede the 19 Sep 2024 release, so likely mid-2024;
  - (b) V3's July 2024 cutoff, which matters for what R1 "knows" when generating the SFT traces;
  - (c) when the SFT prompts were assembled (Dec 2024 to Jan 2025; the math prompts are claimed to be pre-2023);
  - (d) the release on 20 Jan 2025, a hard upper bound.

  The operational rule: **problems first published on or after about Feb 2025 cannot be memorised verbatim**. Problems from Aug 2024 to Jan 2025 are probably absent, but nothing documents it.
- [INF] "Not in the training data" is not the same as "out of distribution". A 2025 or 2026 problem can be verbatim-unseen yet be a near-copy of a pre-2024 AoPS problem that *is* in Qwen's 18T tokens or among the ~395k R1 math prompts. Temporal novelty is necessary but not sufficient.
- [INF] The SFT example in Listing 5 of the R1 paper (minimise Σ√((2k−1)²+a_k²) with Σa_k = 17, find n) is, to my recollection, **AIME 1991 Problem 15**. This would confirm that historical AIME problems are among the distillation prompts. The identification is mine and should be checked against the AoPS wiki.
- [INF] For CoT-resampling studies, the SFT stage matters more than pretraining. The model's reasoning style comes from about 395k R1 math traces. Any problem whose R1 trace sits in the SFT set could be partly reproduced from memory, sentence by sentence.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives a knowledge cutoff for Qwen2.5 or Qwen2.5-14B. Third-party claims (such as "late 2023" or "mid-2024") were not found in primary sources and should not be cited.
- The exact list of prompt sources behind the 395k math SFT samples (AoPS? NuminaMath? Chinese K-12?) is not disclosed beyond "pre-2023 competitions" plus unnamed verifiable sources. The 800k set has never been released.
- The claim that math SFT prompts came "exclusively from pre-2023 competitions" sits uneasily with "a wide range of topics and difficulty levels" and with Qwen-sourced data. I could not verify it independently.

## Known training sources and benchmark contamination in the lineage

### Takeaway
GSM8K and MATH (train), NuminaMath and Chinese K-12 sets are documented in Qwen2.5-Math post-training, and historical competition problems are documented in R1 SFT. Qwen claims to have decontaminated against AIME24, AMC23, MATH and OlympiadBench, yet Qwen2.5 base models verbatim-complete large fractions of MATH-500 and AMC problems. MathArena finds AIME 2024 scores inflated by 10–20 points across many models. Treat MATH-500, AIME ≤2024, AMC ≤2023 and OlympiadBench as contaminated for this model family.

### Cited Findings
- Qwen2.5-Math post-training CoT data uses "GSM8K, MATH, and NuminaMath" plus exclusive K-12 data. Decontamination targets include AIME 24 and AMC 23 (13-gram matching, LCS ratio above 0.6). [V] — [arXiv 2409.12122](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12122)
- R1: the math SFT and RL prompts are "pre-2023 competitions". The authors concede that paraphrases escape the 10-gram filter and that "benchmarks released before 2024 may suffer from contamination issues". [V] — [arXiv 2501.12948v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.12948v2)
- **Wu et al., "Reasoning or Memorization? Unreliable Results of Reinforcement Learning Due to Data Contamination"** (arXiv 2507.10532; v1 14 Jul 2025, final 17 Dec 2025), greedy partial-prompt completion results:
  - **MATH-500, 60% prefix shown (EM of the remainder):** Qwen2.5-Math-7B 54.6%, Qwen2.5-7B 21.2%, Llama3.1-8B 3.8%. Qwen2.5-Math-7B "answers 53.6% of these incomplete problems correctly".
  - **MATH-500, 40% prefix:** 39.2%, 8.2% and 0.6% for the same three models.
  - **AMC, 80% prefix:** Qwen2.5-Math-7B 55.4% vs Llama 4.8%.
  - **AIME 2024, 60% prefix:** 20.0% vs 0.0%.
  - **LiveMathBench (202505):** "Qwen's completion rate drops sharply to 0.0%, consistent with Llama's 0.0%".
  - **R1-Distill was not tested.** The authors propose the synthetic, leakage-free RandomCalculation set. [V] — [arXiv 2507.10532](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10532)
- **Shao et al., "Spurious Rewards: Rethinking Training Signals in RLVR"** (arXiv 2506.10947; Jun 2025, revised Feb 2026): random rewards raise Qwen2.5-Math-7B's MATH-500 score by 21.4 points, against 29.1 points for true rewards. The authors attribute this to GRPO clipping bias amplifying pre-existing "code reasoning" (65% → over 90% frequency), *not* primarily to contamination. The gains fail for Llama3 and OLMo2. Wu et al. read the same phenomenon as evidence of contamination. [V] — [arXiv 2506.10947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10947); [arXiv 2507.10532](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10532)
- **MathArena** (Balunović et al., arXiv 2505.23281; v3 14 Jan 2026; NeurIPS 2025 D&B):
  - "Most models lie above this line on AIME with a margin of 10%−20%, suggesting inflated performance on AIME 2024 due to data contamination". QwQ-32B-Preview outperforms its expected human-aligned score by nearly 60%;
  - "8 problems from AIME 2025 and 1 problem from HMMT 2025 can be found online in a similar form" (Math StackExchange, Quora, Wyzant), but these are "mostly easier problems that do not affect the overall results";
  - R1-Distill-Qwen-32B scored 60.0% on AIME 2025. [V] — [arXiv 2505.23281](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23281); [NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/121385)
- MathArena Apex notes that some 2025 competition problems were public before the evaluated models were released, "but contamination does not appear to be a significant issue at this time". [V, from search snippet] — [MathArena Apex](https://matharena.ai/apex/)
- An identical copy of AIME 2025 Q1 was reported on Quora. [V, from search snippet; secondary] — [greaterwrong comment](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/RDG2dbg6cLNyo6MYT/thane-ruthenis-s-shortform/comment/vxHcdFb3KLWEQbmRv)

### Inferences
- [INF] **Treat as contaminated for R1-Distill-Qwen-14B:** GSM8K, MATH (train and test, including MATH-500), NuminaMath (its sources include AoPS, AMC/AIME and Chinese olympiads), AIME ≤2024, AMC ≤2023, OlympiadBench, Omni-MATH (sourced from AoPS and past olympiads), and any AoPS-wiki problem published before mid-2024.
- [INF] Wu et al. tested Qwen2.5-7B and Math-7B, not 14B. Same pretraining corpus implies a similar memorisation profile for Qwen2.5-14B; larger models usually memorise more. Distillation SFT on top probably erodes verbatim prefix-completion but not the underlying familiarity (see the detection section).
- [INF] AIME 2025 (Feb 2025) is post-release for this model, but MathArena's finding of 8 of 30 problems with near-copies online means it is **not uniformly OOD**. Per-problem screening is needed.

### Gaps
- No published prefix-completion or MIA study targets R1-Distill-Qwen-14B specifically on MATH-500 or AIME. Wang et al. 2510.02386 used R1-Distill-14B, but only in a controlled re-contamination setting.
- The specific AIME 2025 problem numbers with online near-copies were not extracted (they are listed in the MathArena paper's appendix).
- The specific allegation that AIME 2025 problems resemble *older AIME/AoPS* problems, beyond MathArena's online-copy finding, was not verified in a primary source this session.

## Detection methods usable with a local open-weights 14B model, and their reliability

### Takeaway
Every memorisation-based detector (perplexity/LOSS, zlib, Min-K%, Min-K%++, neighbourhood perturbation, even verbatim completion) is **unreliable on reasoning models**. On R1-Distill-Qwen-7B/14B with CoT-format contamination they score AUROC ≈ 50%. Detectors also fail to flag contamination introduced through SFT with answer augmentation. Recent work (2025–26) therefore recommends **temporal (post-release) sourcing as the primary guarantee**, with detectors as weak screening signals only. Positive hits are informative; negative results do not establish cleanliness.

### Cited Findings
- **Wang, Li, Ko, Zhang, "On the Fragility of Benchmark Contamination Detection in Reasoning Models"** (arXiv 2510.02386; v2 2 Mar 2026; ICLR 2026):
  - tests 10 detectors: Verbatim, CDD, Neighborhood, LiRA, Ref, Zlib, Min-K%, Min-K%++, Max-K% and LOSS;
  - Stage I: after GRPO on clean data, "AUROC decreases… from 74.96% to 58.54%" for Min-K%, and LiRA falls from 89.13% to 74.89%. PPO-style clipping "conceals" contamination;
  - Stage II: CoT contamination of **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Qwen-14B and Llama-8B** leaves "AUROC≈50%" for most methods; the best (LiRA) averages 58.74%;
  - recommendations: release intermediate checkpoints, and develop detectors that account for long CoT. [V] — [arXiv 2510.02386](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02386v2); [ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10008591)
- **Zhang, Choi, Li, Wei, "Detecting Distillation Data from Reasoning Models"** (arXiv 2510.04850, 6 Oct 2025):
  - proposes Token Probability Deviation (TBD), which scores the model's *generated* tokens: distilled models produce near-deterministic tokens for seen questions and more low-probability tokens for unseen ones;
  - reports AUC 0.918 and TPR@1%FPR 0.47 on S1 data with Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct;
  - tested on Qwen2.5 7B/14B/32B models fine-tuned in-house, not on R1-Distill. [V] — [arXiv 2510.04850](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04850v1)
- **Samuel, Zhou, Zou** (arXiv 2409.09927, COLING 2025): five detectors on four LLMs show "notable difficulties… detecting contamination introduced during instruction fine-tuning with answer augmentation" and "limited consistencies between SOTA contamination detection techniques". Min-K% has no principled threshold outside AUC-with-oracle settings, and its signal *decreased* after fine-tuning. [V] — [arXiv 2409.09927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09927)
- **Fu, Uzuner, Yetisgen, Xia, "Does Data Contamination Detection Work (Well) for LLMs?"** (arXiv 2410.18966, NAACL 2025 Findings): surveys 50 papers and 8 assumption categories. "MIA approaches based on these three assumptions can have similar performance to random guessing, on datasets used in LLM pretraining". [V] — [arXiv 2410.18966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18966)
- **Prefix-completion / reconstruction** (Wu et al. 2507.10532): works well on the *base* Qwen2.5 (EM of 21–55% on MATH-500 and AMC vs about 0–5% for Llama) and gives a near-zero floor on clean data (LiveMathBench at 0%). It uses greedy decoding without a chat template, and scores by EM and ROUGE-L. [V] — [arXiv 2507.10532](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10532)
- **Perturbation deltas**, MATH-Perturb (Huang et al., arXiv 2502.06453, 10 Feb 2025):
  - 279 perturbed MATH level-5 problems, split into Simple (same method) and Hard (different method needed);
  - o1-mini drops 16.49% and gemini-2.0-flash-thinking 12.9% on P-Hard;
  - models "blindly apply learned problem-solving skills without assessing their applicability to modified contexts";
  - R1-Distill not tested. [V] — [arXiv 2502.06453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06453)
- Background method references [BK, IDs not re-fetched]:
  - Min-K% Prob: Shi et al., arXiv 2310.16789, ICLR 2024;
  - Min-K%++: Zhang et al., arXiv 2404.02936;
  - TS-Guessing (mask a wrong MCQ option or a key word, then ask the model to fill it): Deng et al., arXiv 2311.09783, NAACL 2024;
  - Duan et al., "Do Membership Inference Attacks Work on LLMs?", arXiv 2402.07841, showing near-random MIA on pretraining data;
  - GSM-Symbolic: Mirzadeh et al., arXiv 2410.05229, which templates GSM8K and measures variance and drops.

### Inferences
- [INF] **Method-by-method assessment for our setting** (a local R1-Distill-Qwen-14B, plus base Qwen2.5-14B, which is also open):
  1. **Prefix completion.** Run it on *base Qwen2.5-14B* (no template, greedy) as well as on the distill. The base model carries the pretraining memory without the SFT/CoT format masking it. It is high-precision but low-recall: a hit means contamination, a miss means little. Use an EM or ROUGE-L threshold calibrated against a known-clean set, for example problems written after 2025 or RandomCalculation.
  2. **Min-K% and Min-K%++.** Compute them on base Qwen2.5-14B over the problem text, relative to a matched clean reference set (same style and length, post-2025). Use them only for relative ranking. Wang et al. show they are near-random on the distill for CoT contamination, so do not trust absolute thresholds.
  3. **TS-Guessing.** Designed for MCQ and key-word masking. Weak for free-response math, but masking a key number or phrase in the statement and checking exact recovery is a cheap verbatim probe.
  4. **Perturbation delta.** Accuracy and log-prob on the original vs a semantics-changing variant (MATH-Perturb style) or a numeric re-instantiation (GSM-Symbolic style). A large drop on numeric re-instantiation, or reuse of the original answer on a perturbed problem, flags memorisation. It needs many samples per problem, which a resampling pipeline already produces.
  5. **N-gram overlap with public corpora.** Search candidate problems against AoPS (forums and wiki), Math StackExchange, NuminaMath-1.5, Omni-MATH, OlympiadBench, MATH and the GSM8K train set, plus open web-scale indexes. infini-gram or WIMBD-style indices over open corpora are possible tools [BK]. This is the only check independent of the model. Qwen's actual corpus is closed, so it is a proxy.
  6. **No-reasoning accuracy.** Prefill an empty or closed `<think>\n</think>` block and ask for a direct answer. Correct answers on hard problems without any CoT strongly suggest the answer was memorised. Expect a near-0% baseline on genuinely hard novel problems. I found no paper validating this exact protocol for R1-Distill, so it is an untested heuristic.
  7. **TBD (generated-token probability deviation).** The method most aligned with "was this question in the distillation SFT set". It needs calibration on known-member and known-non-member questions; AIME ≤2023 can serve as likely members and post-Feb-2025 problems as non-members.
- [INF] **Recommended protocol.** Use post-release sourcing as the guarantee, an external near-duplicate search as the main filter, and model-based probes (2, 4, 6, 7 on both the base and distill) as an ensemble red-flag screen. Report the screens transparently rather than claiming proof of cleanliness.

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 paper I found validates "no-thinking accuracy" or perturbation deltas specifically as contamination tests for R1-Distill.
- Min-K%++ and TS-Guessing details and numbers were not re-fetched this session.
- No public n-gram index of Qwen's pretraining corpus or of R1's SFT data exists.

## Near-duplicate risk for new competition problems

### Takeaway
Temporal novelty does not guarantee novelty of content. MathArena found that roughly 27% of AIME 2025 problems (8 of 30) had similar versions online, and DeepSeek explicitly concedes that paraphrases evade its 10-gram filter. Every candidate problem should be screened for semantic near-duplicates against pre-2025 sources, and flagged problems should be excluded or analysed separately.

### Cited Findings
- MathArena: "8 problems from AIME 2025 and 1 problem from HMMT 2025 can be found online in a similar form" (Math StackExchange, Quora, Wyzant). They judged these to be mostly easier problems with little effect on aggregate scores. The paper gives no formal deduplication protocol. [V] — [arXiv 2505.23281](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23281)
- DeepSeek: "the n-gram based decontamination method cannot prevent the paraphrase of testset", and "variations of test set questions or discussions of related problems may still exist on websites that were included in the pre-training corpus". [V] — [arXiv 2501.12948v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.12948v2)
- Qwen decontaminates with 13-gram and LCS ≥ 0.6, which is lexical only. [V] — [arXiv 2409.12122](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12122)
- MATH-Perturb shows that models reuse the solution steps of an original problem on perturbed variants, so a near-duplicate can trigger memorised reasoning even when the answer differs. [V] — [arXiv 2502.06453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06453)

### Inferences
- [INF] **Suggested handling for each candidate problem** (e.g. AIME 2025/2026, HMMT Feb 2025+, USAMO 2025+, MathArena-tracked 2025–26 contests, or newly written problems):
  1. Record the first-publication date and keep only problems after Jan 2025 (stricter: after Feb 2025).
  2. Run an embedding search (math-aware embeddings of the statement plus final answer) and a lexical search against AoPS, MSE, NuminaMath, Omni-MATH, OlympiadBench, MATH and older AIME/AMC/HMMT. Manually review the top-k matches; MathArena reviewed manually too.
  3. Classify each problem as *verbatim/near-verbatim*, *same problem with different numbers*, *same key lemma or technique* (acceptable, since this is just competence) or *novel*. Exclude the first two or analyse them as a separate stratum. This gives a natural contamination control arm for anchor and probe analyses.
  4. Run the model-based screen from the detection section (no-think accuracy, base-model prefix completion, Min-K%++ ranking against clean references). Drop problems that are outliers on any screen.
  5. Optionally create perturbed twins (numbers changed, a constraint altered). If CoT anchors or probe signals are stable across twins, contamination is unlikely to be driving them.
- [INF] Since today is 2026-10-11, more than 18 months of post-release competitions are available, including AIME 2025–2026, HMMT 2025–2026, and the 2025 and 2026 IMO/USAMO. Choosing problems from 2026 adds margin against Aug 2024–Jan 2025 ambiguity and against early leaks of 2025 problems.

### Gaps
- No standard published near-duplicate threshold exists for math problems. The embedding model and threshold must be chosen and validated by hand.
- I did not find a systematic study of AIME 2025 problems resembling *older competition* problems, as opposed to Q&A sites. Such claims circulate informally but remain unverified here.
